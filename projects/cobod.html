Mechatronics Internship at COBOD

COBOD builds large 3D printers for concrete buildings. In the R&D department I ran two projects largely on my own: inline pressure and flow monitoring for the dosing line, and a cleaning concept for the printers' vertical racks.

Pressure and flow monitoring for a dosing line

COBOD's printers add chemicals to the concrete through a dosing line. Dosing errors were only caught by eye after printing. I added inline pressure and flow measurement so the system can see what it is actually delivering.

  • Gathered requirements from the dosing, mechatronics and department leads, and read the chemicals' safety data sheets to set the sensor material requirements.
  • Selected an IO-Link pressure sensor and a turbine flow sensor with a signal converter, and pitched the choice to the department head for budget approval.
  • Built it on an Omron NX102 PLC with a remote EtherNet/IP rack, programmed in Sysmac Studio (structured text, function blocks): a physics-based flow model, two independent flow measurements, sensor monitoring with fault detection and watchdog, and low-level interlocks.
  • Designed a systematic test protocol with measuring cylinders at several flow rates. It showed the pump delivering well below its nominal setting.
Verified

Pressure sensing, EtherNet/IP communication, pump-speed control, the flow model and the dual flow measurement.

Cleaning concept for the Z-axis racks

The printers climb vertical toothed racks. On outdoor sites sand and dust wear the racks out. I developed a cleaning system that can be retrofitted and needs no electronics: brushes driven by the printer's own motion clean the teeth in both directions of travel.

  • Set the design requirements, including removal of most loose debris and cleaning in both directions.
  • Analysed a shared drive principle that takes its motion from the existing climbing gear.
  • Rejected early concepts and, working directly with a brush supplier, developed three viable brush concepts.
  • Chose the bristle material from supplier samples and data sheets, and switched to a cheaper brush when the custom sample cost too much.
  • 3D-printed rack sections to check how the brush engages the teeth, and built a Fusion 360 CAD proof of concept.
  • Handed over at concept stage with a roadmap for the next steps.

Other work during the internship

  • Assembled a complete printer control box and a Z cartridge.
  • Taught myself Omron PLC programming (Sysmac Studio, structured text and ladder) by building two games on COBOD's PLC training rig: Simon Says with random sequences up to 100 rounds, and a reaction-time game with random prompts and delays of 0.5–1.5 s.
  • Tested a newly designed PCB for the printers' controller to check it fixed the previous version's overheating.
  • Helped coordinate the company's move from six sites in Nordhavn to new premises in Taastrup: inventory, packing, logistics and setting up the new facilities.

Evaluation from the Head of R&D: motivation 5 of 5; knowledge, problem solving, learning new things, teamwork and meeting deadlines 4 of 5.
