Terrain-Adaptive Landing Gear for a VTOL Drone

A drone that lands on a slope or on rocks ends up tilted. We built landing gear with three legs that extend until each foot touches the ground, so the drone settles level.

The problem

Multirotor drones need flat ground. On a slope or on rocks the body ends up tilted, and the next take-off is risky because the thrust points along the tilted body, not straight up. Rigid landing legs pass every bump in the terrain straight into the airframe.

The question we set ourselves: can the remaining body tilt after landing be brought below 5° on slopes up to 30° and height differences up to 200 mm, compared with rigid legs on the same Holybro X650?

What we built

We benchmarked four families of existing mechanisms (linkages, linear extension, articulated legs and thrust-assisted designs) and picked linear extension in a weighted concept selection. The final gear has three legs at 0°, 120° and 240°.

  • Each leg is a three-stage telescope. One geared DC motor turns three nested 3D-printed screws, 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 mm of travel per motor turn, about 48 mm/s.
  • A ball-jointed foot carries a contact switch and a compliance spring. When the foot touches the ground the switch stops that leg. No target length is computed: each foot decides its own final length.
  • A VL53L5CX multizone time-of-flight sensor looks at the ground and decides whether the legs need to deploy at all.
  • Two ESP32 microcontrollers on a CNC-milled carrier board, TB6612 motor drivers and encoders run the legs. The gear talks to the Pixhawk 6C flight controller over MAVLink.
  • As installed the gear weighs 1.904 kg; the whole aircraft 4.454 kg, 70.7 % of the X650's 6.3 kg take-off limit.

My part

What I did in this two-person project

I owned the mechatronics and system integration: making the mechanical, electrical and software sides work as one system.

  • Built and wired the electronics from the ground up: the motors and sensors that let each leg sense the ground and move on its own.
  • Wrote the ESP32 firmware that runs the legs in real time, split into leg control and sensing/communication.
  • Designed the custom controller board in KiCad (CNC-milled) that brings sensors, motor drivers and controllers together.
  • Built a PX4/Gazebo software-in-the-loop simulation of the whole drone to compare adaptive and rigid landings on slopes and rough ground, before physical tests.
  • Researched existing mechanisms in books and other sources, which fed the concept choice.

My project partner did the leg CAD, the 3D-print iterations of the leg and the structural FEA. Those are team results on this page, not mine.

Results

Simulation. In my PX4/Gazebo runs the adaptive gear settled at 0.25–1.24° of tilt on uneven terrain, against 6.5–10.5° for rigid legs on the same terrain (one run per terrain case).

Built aircraft. On five terrain setups the legs settled the X650 at 2.1–2.9°: flat ground 2.1°, 100 mm step 2.3°, 23° slope with a step 2.8°, 30° slope 2.6°, and a 30° slope with a 160 mm block 2.9°. These were driven by hand with the rotors unpowered and the drone on a safety line.

Deployment. A full 200 mm extension took 4.17 s on the bench.

Lessons I took from it

  • The flight goal grew the scope and loaded one person. Freeze a testable revision before calibration and combined tests.
  • Design assemblies to be serviceable. Fit changes kept disturbing the wiring.
  • A powered controller board shorted against the conductive carbon-fibre frame. Insulation and mounting are part of the electronics design.

Proposed next steps

  • Automatic deployment triggered by the ToF sensor, then flight validation of the full system, then impact, side-load and buckling cases.
  • A CNC-cut carbon-fibre mounting plate to reduce bending.
  • A single-controller four-layer board with keyed connectors and current feedback to replace the hand-wired two-ESP32 carrier.
